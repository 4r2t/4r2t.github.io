  While my story carries many beats similar to other comeback stories, I believe I bring unique artistic strokes to the pages written so far in my metaphorical book of life. I had originally intended to graduate from Auburn University in 2012 with an Electrical and Computer Engineering degree. After bouncing off of (what I believe was) a Java coding class I reevaluated and set my sights on Electrical Engineering. The robotics! The components! The power! And the math. Lots and lots and lots of math. The major challenged my relationship with math, to the point where I could not overcome Differential Equations. With the language barrier from the only professor who offered the class, and the sheer apparent difficulty increase relative to prior math classes, I failed out spectacularly.

  I had tried to get support, but the minimal resources offered by the university were little help. By that point in my collegiate journey I was on a clear downward trend towards failure. My mental state was non-existent, I had no support from any friends as they had abandoned me the year prior, and I was too ashamed to admit to my family just how much help I needed. I clammed up. I checked out. I just couldn't handle the stress anymore. Not to say that I didn't learn anything while I attended - I learned about so many different cultures, concepts, and stories - most of my classes I very much enjoyed. As much as I was discovering about the world, I was learning about myself. I had musical talent I didn't know existed in my ability to drum! I also had a litany of undiagnosed medical issues that were beginning to reveal themselves.

  It took me years to come to terms with those facts. As it turns out I was dealing with Major Depressive Disorder on and off for most of my adult life. Therapy helped, as well as medication, which enabled me to pursue my Associate's degree from Jefferson State. I was also curious to see if I'd lost any of my mental spark, my aptitude I'd been so prideful about in my youth but had long felt lost after enduring the Auburn gut-punch. I'm proud to say that Jefferson State provided me the opportunity to not only meet, but exceed the expectations I had set for myself. My economics professor Mr. Fisher requested me, out of all possible achieving students, to lead supplementary study lectures for struggling students. I was elated! I may be wrong with regard to the timing but I believe the same semester I was also falling in love with statistics and probability. Partially fueled by my attraction to math, further inflamed by the quality of the instructor, Dr. Schatz, may he rest in peace.

  And as suddenly as this segue, COVID-19 happened. I'd only graduated less than a year prior, with the original intention to follow through on a bridge program to an Accounting degree from the University of Alabama at Birmingham. Instead, as it tends to, life happened and washed those plans away. I became an insurance agent for a short time before being promoted to a field marketing manager. This position challenged me in ways I'd never endured. Turns out the stress incurred made me realize that I also have ADHD, which caused me to reevaluate a large portion of my life, my decisions, even my reason for existence. It's truly brought out the best in me, both figuratively and literally, as my wife gave birth to our daughter Lilly on August 22, 2024.

  Lilly's been my inspiration for so many things, and I believe now is the time for me to finish the Bachelor's component of my journey towards higher education. While a good learner can find opportunity anywhere, the benefits of a Bachelor's degree are unmatched.
